# Ad Craft — what makes a Meta ad win in 2026

## The 2026 reality: creative IS the targeting

Meta's Andromeda delivery engine (fully rolled out late 2025) matches ads to individuals based on creative signals, not audience settings. Consequences:

- Run **broad**. Interest-stacking and narrow audiences are legacy behavior that starves the algorithm.
- Ship **10–15 conceptually distinct creatives** per campaign, not 15 variants of one idea. Each distinct angle reaches a different pocket of buyers. (For a small local budget, 5–8 distinct concepts is the practical floor.)
- Accounts testing 20+ new ads/month materially outperform those testing under 10. Creative refresh every 3–4 weeks; keep 3–5 ads active minimum.
- A "concept" = different angle/hook/format. A "variant" = same idea, new words. Concepts feed Andromeda; variants don't.

## Anatomy of a winning local-service ad

You have ~1.5 seconds of attention. Structure every ad as **Hook → Body → CTA**, one job each:

1. **Hook** (first line of text + first 3 seconds of video/first glance at image): name a specific, felt problem or a specific number. Never open with the company name, logo, or "We are a family-owned…".
2. **Body**: agitate briefly → present the offer → stack proof → remove risk. Short lines. Grade-5 reading level. Write like a contractor talking to a neighbor, not a brand.
3. **CTA**: exactly ONE action (one CTA per asset). For lead campaigns: "Tap below — takes 30 seconds" style. Tell them what happens next ("we'll call you right back") because that sets up the 60-second AI call as a feature, not a surprise.

## Hook bank (patterns, not scripts)

Pick different patterns for different concepts:

| Pattern | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Symptom question | "Is your [specific symptom]?" | "Is your bedroom 10° hotter than your living room?" |
| Number lead | Stat/dollar first | "$2,000 back when you switch to a heat pump." |
| Negation interrupt | Stop/Don't/Avoid | "Don't replace your roof until you read this." |
| Local callout | [City] + homeowners | "Austin homeowners: your neighbors are overpaying for…" |
| Neighbor proof | Someone nearby already did it | "3 homes on [street/area] booked inspections this week." |
| Deadline (real) | Insurance/rebate/season window | "Insurance gives you 60 days after hail. Day 12 is today." |
| Payment reframe | Monthly cost vs total | "A new AC for $89/mo — not $8,900 upfront." |
| Myth-bust | "Everyone thinks X. Actually Y." | "Solar isn't for rich people anymore. Here's the math." |
| Weather/event trigger | Tie to what just happened | "Last Tuesday's hail dropped 1.5-inch stones on [County]." |

## Copy frameworks for the body

- **PAS** (Problem–Agitate–Solve): default for problem-aware buyers (repairs, emergencies).
- **Offer-first**: lead with the deal, justify after. Best when the offer itself is strong (rebate, financing, free named inspection).
- **Proof-story**: one specific customer outcome told in 4–6 lines, then the offer. Best for high-skepticism niches (solar, remodeling).

## Offer engineering

The offer decides CPL more than the copy does. Rank of strength for high-ticket local:

1. **Payment reframe** — "$X/mo" kills the price objection before it forms (mind the Credit special-ad-category framing — say "flexible payment options," avoid loan/approval language).
2. **Real deadline** — rebate expiry, insurance claim window, seasonal booking cutoff.
3. **Risk reversal** — "No claim approved, no charge." "Free until you're booked."
4. **Named free deliverable** — not "free quote" but "free drone roof inspection with adjuster-ready report." A named artifact promises a finding, not a pitch.
5. Generic discount — weakest; use only when the client insists.

## Creative briefs

Every concept gets a brief a videographer or the client could shoot on a phone:

- **Format**: vertical 9:16 video 15–30s (default — highest engagement), static before/after, or 3–5 card carousel (job process / testimonials).
- **Authenticity beats polish**: real job photos, owner-on-camera, crew at work, actual before/afters. Stock imagery and slick brand ads underperform for local trades — people hire people.
- Video brief = scene list with seconds, spoken line or text overlay per scene, and the hook visible in second 0–3 **with captions on** (most feed video plays muted).
- Static brief = exact image description + max 10 words of overlay text.

## Quality bar (check before delivering)

- Would a homeowner in that city stop scrolling at line 1? (If line 1 works without line 2, it's a hook.)
- Is each concept a genuinely different angle?
- Zero personal-attribute violations (talk about the house, not the person).
- Every claim provable by the client. Every deadline real.
- One CTA, and it sets the expectation of a fast callback.
